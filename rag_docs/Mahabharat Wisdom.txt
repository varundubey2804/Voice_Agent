=== ARJUNA'S DOUBT AND OVERCOMING PARALYSIS ===
Character: Arjuna
Context: On the battlefield of Kurukshetra, Arjuna sees his own teachers, cousins, and elders on the opposing side. He drops his bow Gandiva and refuses to fight. He is overwhelmed by grief, confusion, and moral conflict.
Teaching: Krishna does not dismiss Arjuna's feelings. He first listens, then guides through the Bhagavad Gita. The lesson is that feeling stuck or paralyzed by difficult choices is human — not weakness.
Modern Application: When you feel unable to act because every option seems wrong, pause and speak to someone you trust. Break the problem into one small actionable step. Like Arjuna, clarity often comes after honest conversation, not before it.
Keywords: doubt, anxiety, decision paralysis, overwhelm, confusion, stress at work

=== KRISHNA'S TEACHING ON DETACHMENT (GITA 2.47) ===
Verse essence: "Karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana" — You have a right to perform your duty, but not to the fruits of action.
Teaching: Focus on what you can control — your effort, your attitude, your next step. Release obsessive worry about outcomes you cannot guarantee.
Modern Application: At work, give your best to the task without tying your self-worth to promotions or praise. At home, do your part lovingly without demanding specific reactions. This reduces chronic stress and burnout.
Keywords: work stress, burnout, perfectionism, outcome anxiety, motivation

=== KARNA — DIGNITY DESPITE ADVERSITY ===
Character: Karna
Context: Born to Kunti but abandoned, raised as a charioteer's son, denied recognition, mocked for his lineage, yet he mastered archery and fought with unmatched loyalty.
Teaching: Life is not always fair. Karna's story teaches that self-worth need not depend on others' validation. Your effort and character define you.
Modern Application: If you feel undervalued at work or in relationships, remember Karna — continue developing your skills and showing up with integrity. Seek environments that recognize you, but do not let rejection define your value.
Keywords: self-esteem, feeling undervalued, rejection, confidence, imposter syndrome

=== YUDHISHTHIRA — PATIENCE IN ADVERSITY ===
Character: Yudhishthira
Context: Lost his kingdom, endured exile for thirteen years, faced humiliation in the court of Virata, yet never abandoned dharma (righteousness).
Teaching: Endurance through long hardship requires patience, not passive acceptance of injustice. Yudhishthira waited for the right moment while maintaining his principles.
Modern Application: Long-term goals — career changes, healing from grief, rebuilding after failure — require patience. Progress is not always visible daily. Trust the process while taking consistent small steps.
Keywords: patience, long-term stress, grief, waiting, perseverance

=== BHISHMA — SACRIFICE AND BOUNDARIES ===
Character: Bhishma
Context: Took a vow of lifelong celibacy and service to the throne. His sacrifice was noble but also led to complications when he could not act against wrong decisions of the throne he served.
Teaching: Self-sacrifice is noble, but boundaries matter. Helping others should not mean destroying yourself or enabling harm.
Modern Application: If you always say yes at work or in family, you may burn out like Bhishma — bound by your own vows. Learn to set healthy boundaries. Saying no to protect your wellbeing is also dharma.
Keywords: burnout, people pleasing, boundaries, overwork, saying no

=== DRAUPADI — RESILIENCE AFTER HUMILIATION ===
Character: Draupadi
Context: Humiliated in the Kaurava court, she called upon Krishna and ultimately saw justice served, though the path was long and painful.
Teaching: Surviving humiliation and injustice requires inner strength and support. You do not have to endure alone.
Modern Application: If you have faced bullying, harassment, or public shame, Draupadi's story says: your dignity was never truly taken. Reach out for support. Justice and healing take time, but you deserve both.
Keywords: humiliation, bullying, trauma, resilience, self-respect

=== DHRITARASHTRA — BLINDNESS TO TRUTH ===
Character: Dhritarashtra
Context: Physically blind and emotionally blind to his sons' wrongdoing. His attachment prevented him from correcting Duryodhana.
Teaching: Sometimes we avoid painful truths because we fear change. Emotional denial causes more suffering than facing reality.
Modern Application: Are you ignoring a toxic work environment, an unhealthy relationship, or your own mental health decline? Gentle honesty with yourself is the first step toward change.
Keywords: denial, toxic environment, avoidance, self-deception, wake up call

=== THE PANDAVAS IN EXILE — MAKING THE BEST OF WORST CIRCUMSTANCES ===
Context: The Pandavas spent twelve years in forest exile plus one year incognito. They did not give up; they trained, strategized, and prepared.
Teaching: When life forces you into an unwanted situation — job loss, relocation, illness — use the time to grow skills and plan your return.
Modern Application: A setback is not the end of your story. Use difficult periods to learn, rest, and prepare. The Pandavas returned stronger.
Keywords: job loss, setback, exile, starting over, hope

=== GITA ON FEAR (Chapter 2) ===
Teaching: Krishna tells Arjuna that the wise grieve neither for the living nor the dead. Fear often comes from attachment and from imagining worst outcomes.
Modern Application: Anxiety often lives in the future — "what if." Ground yourself in the present. Ask: "What is actually happening right now?" Most fears are stories our mind tells us.
Keywords: fear, anxiety, worry, future anxiety, catastrophizing

=== WORK AS WORSHIP (KARMA YOGA) ===
Teaching: Krishna teaches karma yoga — performing duty without ego, as an offering. Work becomes meaningful when done with sincerity, not for ego alone.
Modern Application: When work feels meaningless, reconnect with the purpose — who does your work help? Do one task today with full attention, as Arjuna finally fought with focus on his dharma alone.
Keywords: motivation, purpose, work meaning, focus, productivity
